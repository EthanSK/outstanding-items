The loop

Four things every turn, and a fifth only when it helps

  1. Capture

    Every request, correction, deferral and “while you're there” aside becomes an item with a permanent OI-n ID. Off-topic reminders are captured too, never refused.

  2. Label

    Each item gets one of nine statuses, and the skill is forbidden from claiming a rung it did not climb. Editing a file is implemented. Only evidence makes it verified.

  3. Show

    The footer is appended to every user-facing reply while anything is open. At most seven open lines and three done lines, so it stays a reminder rather than a report.

  4. Retire

    Finished and cancelled items move into the crossed-out Done section instead of vanishing, so you can audit what happened and why it stopped.

  5. Recommend

    Only when it would genuinely help — you asked, you came back, you sound buried, or a real decision point arrived. One item, one small step, one sentence. Then it stops talking.

Honest status

The difference between done and said

Four of these labels describe four different amounts of proof. Collapsing them is the failure this skill exists to prevent.

Status labels and the evidence each one requires.
Label Means Required evidence
requested You asked for it. Nothing decided or started. None.
planned An approach exists and was stated. Nothing has changed. The approach, in one sentence.
in-progress You explicitly told the agent to start this specific item in this turn. Your fresh start instruction, recorded in the note.
implemented The change was made but not proven to work. The edit or action performed in this task.
verified The change was proven to work. Command output, a passing check, or your confirmation.
waiting-on-you It needs you in person: a press, an approval, a choice, a credential, an acceptance. The exact action, written in the imperative.
blocked A genuine external impasse, after every safe in-scope route was tried. The blocker and what was already attempted.
reminder An Intentional reminder: parked on purpose, no execution request, no deadline. None. It waits until you give it one.
dropped Deliberately not doing it. Who decided, and why.

Waiting on you is not blocked

“Blocked” reads as nothing to be done, so it gets skipped for a week. If the only missing thing is your click, your key, your yes, or a paste into another conversation, the ledger says waiting-on-you and writes the action in the imperative — click approve in the deploy UI, not needs approval.

blocked is reserved for a real external wall, and it has to carry two facts rather than one: what stopped things, and what was already tried to get around it.

A reminder is not a neglected request

Something you parked deliberately is a reminder. It stays visible, it is never started, it never retires itself into Done, and nobody comments on how long it has been sitting there. That is the entire point of parking it.

It also never competes to be the recommended next action — unless you ask for it, or something makes it genuinely urgent, at which point it becomes a normal request.

Outsource your memory — a curated work experience

The second half: what to actually do

A list tells you what is outstanding. It does not tell you what to pick up when you sit down with eleven open items and forty minutes. That is the other half of this skill.

What it weighs

Judgement, held together. Not a score, and never shown as one.
Factor The question it asks
Dependencies Does finishing this make other things possible? Is anything already waiting on it?
Momentum Are you already inside this file, this idea, this mood? Restarting is the expensive part.
Effort vs value What is the smallest thing with a real payoff? Twenty minutes with an outcome beats three hours toward a milestone.
Availability Can you actually choose it? blocked items are filtered out. A waiting-on-you item may be ideal because the suggested action belongs to you.
Urgency Real consequences only. Age is not urgency, and neither is the length of the list.
Load How much are you carrying right now? After a long push the honest answer is a small thing, or nothing.
Kindness Would a thoughtful colleague say this out loud right now, or leave you alone?

And what keeps it bearable

  • One item, one small step. The twenty-minute version, so starting is cheap and stopping is allowed. No frameworks, no scores, no numbered plans.
  • Not every turn. Most replies carry no recommendation at all. A footer that advises constantly stops being read by turn four.
  • It never edits the ledger. Nothing is dropped, reordered, merged, hidden or deprioritised out of existence because it was not the thing chosen. Recommending is a comment, not an edit.
  • Your priorities win. Say what you want to do and it stops — immediately, without a counter-proposal and without raising it again next turn.
  • Calibrated, not confident. If it is a close call it says so and names the alternative. “Nothing here needs you tonight” is a legitimate answer.

When a footer is not enough

Two escape hatches, both cautious

The list outgrows the reply

Past seven open items, twenty items in total, or a request for the whole list, the ledger moves to one task-owned outstanding-items.json. The footer's Full ledger link opens a private local HTML editor instead of dumping the raw file into your browser.

Open items come first in a deliberately quiet list. A resting row is just its checkbox and task text; click the text to create an inline editor, with no permanent blank input underneath it. Drag the revealed grip or use keyboard move controls to reorder. Checking a task complete moves it to the bottom and shows a temporary Undo snackbar. Explicitly transferred work remains read-only under Owned elsewhere, with its original status and destination intact. The page and the agent both edit the same JSON file, with atomic writes and revision checks, so there is no competing browser or Markdown ledger.

It asks first. It proposes a path, prefers a task scratch directory, and offers to exclude the JSON and local runtime files from Git. The editor starts only when requested, binds to 127.0.0.1, requires its random URL token, stores nothing in the browser, and makes no outbound request.

Another task needs to know

A related conversation is resolved once — visible title plus stable ID — and stored in the registry. After that the reference is reused, never re-derived.

Only relevant changes travel: something that changes the other task's scope, unblocks it, or invalidates an assumption. Deltas are additive, one change each, and never reorder or overwrite what that task already tracks.

If the harness exposes no tools to read or message other tasks, it says so, records the relationship as registered (manual), and gives you the exact text to paste. A send that fails is reported and the registry entry is kept.

Install

Two directories and a copy command

Everything installs from the checked-out repository. No package manager, no build step, and no network access at any point.

The script

git clone https://github.com/EthanSK/outstanding-items.git
cd outstanding-items
sh scripts/install.sh --dry-run
sh scripts/install.sh

The dry run prints a per-file plan and writes nothing. Without a target it installs for each harness whose directory already exists, and skips the others with a note. It validates manifest paths and resolved boundaries, refuses symbolic-link traversal, never runs a recursive delete, and will not overwrite a locally modified skill unless you explicitly use --force.

Or by hand

# Codex
mkdir -p ~/.codex/skills/outstanding-items
cp -R skill/outstanding-items/. ~/.codex/skills/outstanding-items/

# Claude Code
mkdir -p ~/.claude/skills/outstanding-items
cp -R skill/outstanding-items/. ~/.claude/skills/outstanding-items/

One canonical source, copied twice. The SKILL.md frontmatter is valid for both harnesses, and the supported Codex interface metadata alongside it is simply ignored by Claude Code. Manual copies do not get the install manifest; use the installer for conflict-aware updates and hash-checked uninstalling.

Make it fire without asking

Harnesses discover skills from the description field, which is why the trigger phrases live there. A global rule makes invocation more reliable. It is optional, and it does not make invocation certain.

## Outstanding items
Use the `outstanding-items` skill in any task where I
make more than one request. Capture unrelated asides
too, and end every user-facing reply with the
Outstanding footer. Never label something `verified`
without evidence you observed in this task.

Append to ~/.codex/AGENTS.md or ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md.

Check it

sh scripts/check.sh
sh scripts/check.sh --installed

Needs only sh and python3. It validates required files, frontmatter and YAML basics, internal links, site base paths, the synthetic-identifier rule, honesty guards, and the ledger migration/edit/check/reorder lifecycle. No third-party packages, no outbound network requests.

To remove it again: sh scripts/uninstall.sh deletes only the files it installed, then removes directories only when they are already empty.

Limits

What this deliberately is not

These are the assumptions people arrive with. All of them are wrong, and it is better to say so on the homepage than in an issue thread later.

  • Not execution permission. The ledger is memory. Listing, ranking, suggesting or sharing an item never authorises the agent to start, resume, investigate, research, prepare, route or complete it. Only your current message can name what begins, and that authority ends with the response turn.
  • Not a background daemon. Installation schedules and starts nothing. The optional Full ledger is an explicit per-ledger loopback process that you can stop without touching its data.
  • Not a cross-task message bus. There is no broker, no queue and no inbox. Propagation happens only through tools the harness already exposes.
  • Not a database. The only durable ledger is one plain JSON file at a path you approve first. The HTML is a live view of that file, not another store.
  • Not guaranteed invocation. Harnesses decide when to load a skill from its description. A global rule makes it likelier, never certain.
  • Not a task discovery service. Without task tools it cannot find, read or message another conversation. It says so and hands you the text.
  • Not networked. The skill, the installer and this website make no outbound requests. There is no analytics and there are no cookies.
  • Not a priority system, and not a plan. The next-action recommendation is a judgement made from what you said in this task, offered once and dropped if ignored. It does not know your deadlines, your week, or what you have the appetite for — and it will not pretend otherwise.

Questions

Reasonable objections

Is this just a to-do list?

A to-do list is something you maintain. This is something the agent maintains from what you already said, and shows back without being asked. The point is not storage — it is that you never have to restate the list to get accurate work.

Does it work in both Codex and Claude Code?

Yes, from one canonical source. Both read a SKILL.md with name and description frontmatter, so the same file installs into ~/.codex/skills/ and ~/.claude/skills/. The Codex packaging file that sits alongside it is ignored by Claude Code.

What happens to my unrelated reminders?

They are captured and left alone, under their own label: reminder. The skill is explicitly told never to refuse a reminder for being off-topic, never to start it, never to pad it out with questions, and never to retire it on your behalf. It also never gets promoted into the “what next” recommendation unless you ask for it.

Will it really tell my other conversation?

Only if the harness you are running exposes tools to read or message other tasks, and only if the call succeeds. Otherwise it registers the relationship, labels it registered (manual), gives you the exact text to paste, and keeps the item open as waiting-on-you until you say it landed. It is never allowed to imply a message was delivered when it was not.

Does it write files into my repository?

Only after asking, and only past the overflow threshold. It proposes a path, prefers a scratch directory the harness provides, and offers a .git/info/exclude entry so the file cannot be committed by accident. Decline and the whole ledger stays in the conversation.

Is the “what next” bit going to nag me?

No, and that is written into the contract rather than left to taste. It offers a recommendation when you ask, when you come back after a gap, when you sound overloaded, or at a real decision point — and stays silent otherwise. It is one item, one small step and one sentence. If you say you would rather do something else, it agrees and does not raise it again.

Why is waiting-on-you a separate label?

Because blocked quietly means “nothing to be done”, and things that only needed thirty seconds of your attention were disappearing into it. If the missing ingredient is a person — an approval, a credential, a choice, a paste into another conversation — the ledger names the exact action instead. blocked then means what it should: a real external wall, with the attempted route recorded next to it.

Can I change how it behaves?

It is Markdown. Fork it, rewrite the description with your own trigger phrases, swap the status vocabulary for your pipeline, move the thresholds. Then run the checks — they are structural, so they keep working after the prose changes.
